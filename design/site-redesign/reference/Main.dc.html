What do you need to know?

Pick a question. The answers come from a real run on the sample part, using an example shop’s prices.

A lathe can make it. Can yours?

The part is round, so the best way to make it is on a lathe. Tell ScaleCAD which machines you have and it checks the part’s size, about 2 cm each way, against every one. If it’s missing information, it says “unknown” instead of guessing yes.

Two problems. Neither one stops a lathe.

ScaleCAD checks the design against each way of making it. A problem for one method doesn’t rule out the others. Fix the design or pick another method, then check again.

Injection moldingStops molding One wall is too straight to slide out of a mold. It needs at least a 1° angle. Fix: angle that wall slightly, or don’t mold it.
3-axis millingStops 3-axis The cutting tool can’t reach about 6 in 10 of the part’s surfaces from above. Fix: use a lathe or a 5-axis mill.

Before your first part.

What counts as a check?

Each part you upload is one check. You get the full answer every time: can it be made, what’s wrong with it, how long it takes and what it costs.

What files can I upload?

STEP, STL or IGES, the 3D formats almost every CAD program can save. Using SolidWorks, CATIA, NX or Creo? Save a copy as STEP first.

Is this a quote from a supplier?

No. It’s an estimate built from the part and your shop’s numbers. Use it to decide and to negotiate. Your team still signs off before anything gets made.

What if I don’t have all the details?

ScaleCAD fills the gaps with standard guesses and marks every one. It never treats missing information as a yes. Add your own machines or prices anytime and check again.

Can it run on our own servers?

Yes, as part of a pilot. We work through your security and data rules with you before you share any sensitive files.
